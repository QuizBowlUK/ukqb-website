title: Glossary of quizbowl jargon
---

Over the years, the quizbowl community has developed its own somewhat specific jargon. While its knowledge is certainly non-essential to play the game, it is useful to be familiar with some of the commonly used terms, explained below.

**Bonus/bonus set:** A set of three questions linked by some theme, which are awarded to a team after they correctly answer a tossup. Unlike on University Challenge, bonuses are worth 10 points in quizbowl, and there are no music or picture bonuses. Teams may confer on bonuses, but not tossups. Most bonus sets will have an easy, medium and hard part but not necessarily in that order.

**Bracket:** Many tournaments have too many teams for them all to play each other; in this case, teams will play in a ‘prelim bracket’ in the morning against a proportion of the field, and then play teams with roughly the same number of wins as them in the afternoon. The teams with the most wins in a prelim bracket will play in the ‘top bracket’.

**Briticise:** The act of getting an American question set and replacing/editing questions to make it more suitable to a British audience. This may involve adding more questions on British content and reducing the amount of questions on American content and/or making questions on American content easier and questions on British content harder.

**BSQC:** British Student Quizbowl Championships. The most prestigious collegiate tournament of the year in the UK which teams must qualify to participate in.

**Bounceback:** Converting a tossup which has been negged by the other team

**Cap/Field cap:** The maximum number of teams which can play in a given tournament: constraints on rooms and number of people willing to moderate may lead to small field caps.

**Carding:** Technique for retaining information for quiz through the use of flashcards. Physical cards or digital programs such as Anki are used by some quizzers. Can also refer to general quiz study and “get carding” is often used as an ironic term for learning more about a topic.

**Chimera:** A team consisting of players from multiple universities. Chimeras may sometimes be used when a team would otherwise have very few players or to ensure that the number of teams is within the field cap. Not all closed tournaments allow chimeras.

**Clear:** A set is clear when there are no further mirrors of that set worldwide. Clear sets may be discussed publicly; questions from unclear sets must not be discussed publicly or outside dedicated servers/channels to avoid spoiling questions for future mirrors.

**Closed:** A tournament which can only be played by teams of current university students.

**Chestnut:** A fact, broadly speaking. In the original sense, “chestnuts” are clues which are oft-repeated and therefore good to learn; the term is now mostly used tongue-in-cheek. “Chestnut\!” can also be used as an exclamation of surprise.

**Distribution/Distro:** The way in which different topics are spread in a given packet. Distributions are usually described in the form X/Y, where X and Y are numbers; this means the number of tossups and bonuses respectively of a category that will appear in an average packet.

**Dot:** The dot scale is the main difficulty scale for quizbowl tournaments. One dot ● tournaments (e.g. ACF Fall, British Novice) are intended for novices, two dot ●● tournaments (e.g. ACF Winter) are of medium difficulty, three dot ●●● tournaments (e.g. ACF Regionals) are hard, and four dot ●●●● tournaments (e.g. ICT) are very hard.

**Field**: The set of players at a tournament. “Field strength” refers to the overall skill level of the players at the tournament, with stronger fields meaning competition is higher.

**Grail:** The act of one team getting all of the tossups in a match. This happens very, very rarely.

**Housewrite:** A tournament that is hosted at a university, with questions mostly written by members (and alumni) of that university. COOT (Cambridge and Oxford Open Tournament) is the longest running example in the UK.

**Mirror:** A tournament that is held on questions written by people other than the moderators of the tournament. Most British quizbowl tournaments are written in America and mirrored in the UK and some tournaments, such as ACF Fall, receive separate mirrors in the north and south of the UK to reduce travel times for teams and reduce pressure on field caps.

**Moderator/Mod:** The official in charge of reading questions and scoring games.

**Neg:** The act of interrupting a tossup before it is finished, and giving an incorrect answer. Performing a neg loses your team five points, and means that they cannot buzz again on that tossup.

**Open:** A tournament that is open to everyone: current university members, university alumni and people who have never been to university. These tournaments often have more challenging fields than collegiate tournaments.

**Packet:** The set of questions that each quizbowl match is played on. Most packets consist of 20 tossups and bonuses though this may differ in certain tournaments. Most quizbowl sets will also include a packet of spare questions in case a match ends in a draw or a question is thrown out.

**Packet submission:** Tournaments that require teams to write questions (usually half a packet) in order to play, or charge a fee for not doing so.

**Power:** A particularly early correct buzz in a tossup, which earns the player more points. Powers usually are worth 15 points, and correct buzzes ‘out of power’ are worth 10\. Not all tournaments have powers; the tournament director will usually make this clear at the start of the day.

**PPG:** Points per game; the number of points you have earned through buzzing divided by the number of games you have played.

**Prompt:** An answer to a question that is not specific enough, but not incorrect. For example, a team answering ‘King Henry’ to a question about Henry VIII would be prompted, but a team saying ‘Henry III’ would not. Prompts may also be directed, meaning the mod asks the player for a specific extra piece of information. For example, a question on “symphonies by Beethoven” may prompt on “symphonies” alone by asking “by what composer?

**Pyramidal:** One of the core principles of quizbowl, questions being pyramidal means that tossups should start with relatively obscure clues that reward in-depth knowledge of the topic, and then become progressively easier until the final line, the ‘giveaway’, which most teams are likely to know.

**Restricted:** A tournament which is intended for newer players and which players who have performed sufficiently well at previous tournaments may not play. Generally, 1 dot and 1.5 dot tournaments are restricted, with different requirements for each.

**Set:** The collection of questions which each tournament is played on. Each packet within a set will comply with that tournament’s distribution.

**Stock/Stock Clue:** A clue or a concept which is frequently asked about, due to genuine (or perceived) real-world or curriculum importance.

**Tossup:** Question on which teams can buzz on to earn points, similar to starters on University Challenge; correctly answering a tossup will also give a team a chance to answer a set of bonuses. Tossups are significantly longer than University Challenge starters: most are between five and ten lines long, and are pyramidal. There are no tossups that involve looking at pictures or listening to music, although some may describe artworks or musical pieces.

**Tournament Director (TD):** The official in charge of a whole tournament. Any protests about moderator decisions should be directed to the tournament director, and their word is ultimately final.

**Safeguarding Officer:** In a tournament, the safeguarding officer is the official responsible for making sure all participants feel safe and included. Anyone with concerns about safety or inclusion should be directed to them. In the wider UK quizbowl community, this also refers to officers elected to the UKQB Committee who are responsible for safety and inclusion within the community.

**Scrim:** An impromptu online practice session on the website QBreader,

**Vulch:** Because questions are pyramidal, and tossups become easier towards the end of the question, teams will usually wait until the final sentence of a tossup before buzzing after the other team has negged; not waiting until the end of the tossup in such situations is called vulching.

**YGK:** Stands for “You Gotta Know”. Often used ironically in response to obscure or overly niche clues, much like the similar phrase “get carding”.
